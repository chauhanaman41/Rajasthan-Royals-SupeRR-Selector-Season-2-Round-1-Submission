## 4. Open Methodological Limitations & Empirical Deficiencies

As documented in Section 7 of the report, the rankings must be interpreted subject to the following structural limitations:
1. **Opponent Offset Positive Drift:** Batters face stronger bowling (+0.005547 drift) and bowlers face weaker batting (-0.003321 drift) under high pressure.
2. **Dot-Ball Credit Imbalance:** Heavy reward for dot balls distorts bowling evaluations in low-scoring games.
3. **Two Expectations Discrepancy:** Net contextual baseline $\mu_{\text{ctx}} \approx 0.95$ differs from gross scoring rates of 1.33 to 1.35 runs per ball.
4. **$c_{\text{wkt}}$-$P$ Overlap:** Mathematical construction couples pressure intensity with wicket valuation.
5. **Floored Bowler Prior:** $\tau^2_{\text{SH}} = 0.000100$ induces near-total shrinkage, compressing bowler differentials into statistical ties.
6. **Pacer Wicket Dependence:** Fast bowlers (Shami, Cummins) derive over 100% of net rating from wickets (+0.644890 / +0.683713), with negative run terms.
7. **Batter T20I Composition:** Certain top batters accumulate samples in T20Is, linked through 61 shared IPL-T20I players.
8. **Extreme Chase Dependence:** 75.0% of high-pressure deliveries occur in Innings 2, with top-order batters accumulating 90.4% to 99.7% of HP volume chasing.
9. **Static vs Dynamic Wicket Value:** Tactical adaptation alters wicket values beyond static expectations.
10. **Data Horizon Cutoff:** Strict historical boundary at April 2026.
